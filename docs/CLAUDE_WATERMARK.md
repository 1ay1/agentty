# The Claude Text Watermark — A Complete Technical Dossier

> Research compiled August 2026 from primary sources: Anthropic's official
> post, the SynthID-Text *Nature* paper (2024), Scott Aaronson's writings,
> ETH SRI Lab's adversarial analysis, the EU AI Act legal text, and the
> C2PA specification. This is a standalone reference, **not** agentty
> internals — it documents an external phenomenon that affects any output
> produced by recent Claude models (including via this tool).

---

## 0. TL;DR

Since **August 2, 2026**, text generated by new Claude models carries an
**invisible statistical watermark** — Anthropic's deployment of Google
DeepMind's **SynthID-Text** (the *Tournament sampling* scheme published in
*Nature*, 2024), itself a descendant of **Scott Aaronson's 2022
Gumbel-Softmax scheme** built at OpenAI. It is driven by **EU AI Act
Article 50(2)**, applied **globally, with no opt-out**. Separately,
generated **files** (images/PDFs/SVGs) get **C2PA** signed provenance
metadata.

Get these facts right — most online commentary does not:

- **It is NOT hidden Unicode.** No zero-width joiners, no non-breaking
  spaces, no invisible characters. *"Nothing is added to the text and there
  are no hidden characters."* — Anthropic.
- **It costs nothing** — no extra tokens, negligible latency, same price.
- **It is not traceable to you** — provider-level key, carries zero
  identifying information about person, org, or chat.
- **It is weak-to-absent on code, short text, and factual text**, and is
  **removed by full paraphrase**.
- The "Claude watermark remover" tools that strip zero-width characters are
  addressing a **different, unrelated technique** and cannot touch Claude's
  actual watermark.

## 1. Lineage — three generations of one idea

### Gen 1 — Aaronson's Gumbel-Softmax (OpenAI, summer 2022)

The founding insight, in Aaronson's own words:

> "Replace some of the entropy with the output of a pseudorandom function,
> in a way that encodes a statistical signal, which someone who knows the
> key of the PRF could later detect… The crucial point is that the quality
> isn't degraded at all, because we aren't changing the model's
> probabilities for tokens, but only *how we use* the probabilities."

The Gumbel-max trick: to sample a token, draw `U_w ~ Uniform(0,1)` per
vocabulary word and pick `argmax_w (log p_w + Gumbel noise)`. Watermarking
makes those `U_w` a **pseudorandom function of (key, preceding context)**
instead of true randomness. The pivotal detection statistic is
`Y_t = U_{t, w_t}` — the score of the token actually chosen, which trends
high under the watermark.

OpenAI **never deployed it** — feared customers would leave over
watermarking. (Aaronson's colleagues Sam Gunn, Miranda Christ, Or Zamir
later extended this into *cryptographically undetectable* watermarks.)

### Gen 2 — Kirchenbauer et al. "Red-Green List" (UMD, Jan 2023)

The other major family (paper: *"A Watermark for Large Language Models"*,
arXiv 2301.10226). Before each token:

1. Hash the preceding token(s) → seed a PRNG.
2. Partition the vocabulary into a **green list** (fraction γ) and a **red
   list**.
3. Add a bias **δ** to green-token logits (soft) — or forbid red entirely
   (hard).

Watermarked text over-uses green tokens. Detection counts green fraction
and computes a **z-score / interpretable p-value**. Its documented weakness
— **low-entropy sequences** (e.g. "Barack" → "Obama" is near-deterministic;
biasing it either fails or wrecks fluency) — is the **root cause of every
watermark's difficulty with code and facts.**

### Gen 3 — SynthID-Text (Google DeepMind, *Nature*, Oct 2024)

The first **production** scheme (shipped in Gemini App/Web, open-sourced).
**Anthropic adopted this exact scheme for Claude.** It generalizes both
predecessors — this is the one that matters.

## 2. How SynthID-Text actually works

Paper defaults: **m = 30 tournament layers**, **Bernoulli(0.5)** g-value
distribution, **Bayesian** scoring function.

### Embedding — *Tournament sampling*

For each token position:

1. The LLM produces its normal probability distribution over the
   vocabulary — **left unchanged**.
2. Draw candidate tokens from that true distribution.
3. A **watermarking key** + a sliding window of the **preceding tokens**
   (context depth h ≈ 3–4, larger than Red-Green's h=1) seed a pseudorandom
   **g-value function**: each candidate gets a g-value ∈ {0,1}, Bernoulli(0.5).
4. Run a **single-elimination tournament** over **m layers**: pair
   candidates, the higher g-value wins each match (ties → higher model
   probability), advance until one token remains. Emit it.

Winners systematically carry higher g-values, so the emitted sequence is
**statistically biased toward g = 1**, keyed to `(key, context)`. But every
candidate was already a legitimate sample from the model's own
distribution, so **no single choice degrades quality**.

ETH SRI Lab's decomposition is the clearest structural description:

> **SynthID-Text = LeftHash (h=3) Red-Green + increased context size +
> tournament sampling + caching.** Tournament sampling "generalizes the
> boosting of green tokens to *variable* logit biases."

### Distortionary vs. non-distortionary

SynthID supports both. With **repeated-context masking** parameter **K = 1**
it achieves **single-sequence non-distortion**: over one generation the
watermark is provably distribution-preserving (the marginal token
distribution is unchanged). Raising K strengthens the mark but reintroduces
distortion and cost.

### Production integration — speculative sampling

The paper's real engineering contribution: an algorithm that composes
tournament sampling with **speculative sampling** (draft-model
acceleration) so watermarking adds **negligible latency** in a real serving
stack. This is why it's "production-ready" where prior schemes were not.

### Detection

- Runs **without the LLM** — cheap, just the key + the scoring function.
- **Mean score** detector: statistical guarantee, controllable false
  positive rate (Anthropic mentions a "weighted mean detector" with
  rigorously controlled FPR).
- **Bayesian** detector: higher power, but needs training data and becomes
  a *hybrid* watermark + post-hoc detector (it learns the specific model's
  watermarked distribution vs. the human-text distribution).
- Longer text → more token choices → more accumulated evidence → higher
  confidence. Short passages fall below threshold.

## 3. The two mechanisms — do not conflate them

| | **Text watermark** | **C2PA metadata** |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Generated **text** | Generated **files** (PNG/JPG/SVG/PDF) |
| Location | *Inside* the content (word choices) | *Alongside* the file (signed manifest) |
| Cryptographic | No — statistical | **Yes** — digitally signed, tamper-evident |
| Survives copy-paste | **Yes** | N/A (bound to file) |
| Survives re-save / screenshot / format convert | Yes | **No** — trivially stripped |
| Survives heavy paraphrase | **No** | N/A |
| Traceable to a user | No | No (signer = provider) |

**C2PA** (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity; Adobe, Arm,
BBC, Intel, Microsoft, Truepic) embeds a cryptographically signed
**manifest** — assertions about origin + edit history, plus content hashes,
signed by the provider's key with a trusted timestamp. Any post-signing
edit invalidates the signature. But it's **metadata**: re-encoding,
screenshotting, or converting the file **drops it entirely**. Standard C2PA
limitation.

## 4. Robustness & attacks (ETH SRI Lab adversarial study)

The most rigorous independent analysis. Four findings:

1. **Presence is detectable (black-box).** You cannot hide *that* a
   SynthID watermark exists — a Red-Green presence test passes with p ≈ 0,
   because the g-value bias is consistent for a fixed preceding context.
   (Requires correctly estimating the context size / cache parameter.)

2. **Spoofing is hard** (forging fake watermarked text to frame the
   provider). Each SynthID component raises the bar: larger context, then
   tournament sampling, then caching drive spoofing success from ~82% (SOTA
   Red-Green) down to **~4%**. But a larger attacker query budget
   (30k → 90k) reverses much of it (back to ~15%) — so spoofing is *costlier*,
   not impossible.

3. **Spoofing leaves clues.** Learning-based spoofers (stealing /
   distillation) imprint detectable artifacts; a "clue detector" flags them
   with calibrated FPR.

4. **Scrubbing is easy** (removing the watermark while preserving meaning).
   This is the practically important weakness: off-the-shelf paraphrasers
   already scrub SynthID at **>90%** success on 1000-token texts; assisted
   by watermark-stealing it approaches **~100%**. Aaronson conceded the same
   in 2022: *"ask ChatGPT for your term paper in French and stick it into
   Google Translate."* Tournament sampling's g-values appear **more**
   sensitive to rewrites than plain Red-Green.

**Net:** robust against passive copy-paste and light editing; **not** robust
against a motivated adversary who paraphrases or round-trip-translates.

## 5. Why this barely matters for code (and this tool)

Reasoning directly from the mechanism:

- **Code is low-entropy.** Identifiers must match declarations, syntax is
  rigid — there are few "equally good" token choices for the tournament to
  bias. Anthropic confirms code has "generally less watermarking," mostly
  only in **comments**.
- **Formatters are an adversarial attack.** The first `prettier` /
  `clang-format` / `rustfmt` / `gofmt` pass rewrites token boundaries and
  **destroys** whatever token-level signal survived.
- **Short things read clean.** A one-line fix or a commit *subject* is below
  the detection threshold entirely.
- **Prose carries it.** Plan summaries, PR/commit *bodies*, design docs, and
  chat explanations mark well. An Anthropic engineer claimed you "could
  check if a PR was generated by Claude Code" — overstated: the PR **body**
  marks, the **diff** does not.

For a codebase like this one, watermark exposure on the **code itself is
near-zero**; it's modest only on long generated **prose**.

## 6. Why now — the legal driver

- **EU AI Act, Article 50(2):** *"Providers of AI systems… generating
  synthetic audio, image, video or **text** content, shall ensure that the
  outputs… are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as
  artificially generated or manipulated."*
- Operationalized by the **Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated
  Content**, in application **August 2, 2026**. Models placed on the market
  *before* that date get a grace period to **December 2, 2026**.
- No single technique is mandated as sufficient; watermark **and** metadata
  is the pragmatic combination.
- Anthropic applied it **globally** (not EU-only) — reportedly lacking a
  durable region-scoping mechanism at inference time.
- Signed by **most major labs** — this is an industry-wide obligation, not
  a Claude quirk.

## 7. Limitations that matter in practice

- **A hit means "processed," not "authored."** It cannot distinguish Claude
  *writing* the text from Claude *proofreading / translating / formatting*
  human text. Do not treat a detector result as a verdict.
- **Absence proves nothing:** pre-August models, short text, paraphrase, or
  stripped metadata all read clean.
- **No public detection API yet** (an Anthropic engineer says one "you can
  use yourself" is coming). Tension: the Code of Practice wants third-party
  detection, but an open detector lets adversaries iterate paraphrases until
  the mark vanishes.
- **The model isn't aware** it's being watermarked — applied in the sampling
  pipeline *below* the model, so it cannot be prompted away.
- **The credible escape hatch is open-weight models you run yourself** — a
  sampling-time watermark cannot survive an operator who controls the
  sampler.
- **Older Claude models** (pre-Aug 2 releases) are mid-transition, no firm
  date.

## 9. Opting out **in agentty**

### 9.0 The one fact that governs everything

**agentty cannot disable the watermark, and no API client ever can.** The
mark is applied by Tournament sampling *inside Anthropic's inference
server*, at the token-sampling step, on Anthropic's hardware (§2). agentty
is an HTTP/2 client (`src/io/http.cpp`): it sends a request to
`api.anthropic.com` and receives tokens that are **already sampled and
already marked** over SSE. There is **no request header, no body
parameter, no OAuth trick** that turns it off — Anthropic states there is
**no opt-out** and the model itself is unaware of it.

So "turn it off in agentty" can only mean one of two real things:

- **(A) Don't route through Claude** — use a model/provider that Anthropic
  doesn't watermark. *This is the only clean, reliable option.*
- **(B) Scrub the response after the fact** — the paraphrase attack from
  §4. Unreliable, destroys code, and off-topic for a coding agent. Not
  recommended, and agentty deliberately does not ship it (see §9.4).

> ⚠️ **Caveat (see §6):** most major labs signed the *same* EU code of
> practice. Routing to OpenAI or Google models trades Claude's watermark
> for **that provider's** watermark. The genuinely unmarked path is a model
> **whose sampler you control** — i.e. an **open-weight model you run
> locally** (Ollama / llama.cpp). That is the only configuration where
> *no* sampling-time watermark can be present, because you own the sampler.

### 9.1 agentty already has the levers — no code change needed

Provider routing is **registry-driven** (`src/provider/selection.cpp`,
`src/provider/dispatch.cpp`). Anything that isn't the Anthropic preset goes
through the OpenAI-compatible or Ollama transport. The built-in
non-Anthropic routes named in `dispatch.cpp`:

> `openai · groq · openrouter · together · cerebras · llama.cpp · ollama ·
> copilot · codex (→ ChatGPT) · custom host[:port]` — plus any external
> **ACP agent** you configure.

Select one per session with `--provider` (`src/runtime/main.cpp`), or
persist it as `provider` in settings (`~/.agentty/…`, loaded by
`load_settings`).

### 9.2 The recommended opt-out: local open-weight model (zero watermark)

Because you own the sampler, a local model carries **no** sampling-time
watermark from anyone.

```sh
# 1. Run a local OpenAI-compatible / Ollama server
ollama serve &
ollama pull qwen2.5-coder:7b        # or a larger coder model

# 2. Point agentty at it — no API key needed (AuthStyle::None)
agentty --provider ollama --model qwen2.5-coder:7b

# llama.cpp server instead? it's just a custom OpenAI-compatible host:
agentty --provider llama.cpp --model <id>          # or
agentty --provider localhost:8080 --model <id>     # raw custom endpoint
```

This is the **only** configuration that is watermark-free by construction.
Everything else depends on that provider's compliance choices.

### 9.3 Other non-Claude routes (traded, not eliminated)

```sh
agentty --provider openrouter  --model <id>   # OPENROUTER_API_KEY / --key
agentty --provider groq        --model <id>
agentty --provider together    --model <id>
agentty --provider openai      --model gpt-...  # OpenAI's OWN mark may apply
```

Key precedence (`resolve_auth_for`): `--key` > saved in-app key > provider
env chain > `OPENAI_API_KEY`. Gateways that reject `Authorization: Bearer`
take `--auth-header X-API-Key` (`set_custom_auth_header`). These reach a
non-Anthropic sampler, so **Claude's** watermark is absent — but see §6:
you may inherit the destination provider's watermark instead.

### 9.4 What agentty should — and should not — do about this

**Recommended product stance (honest, minimal, non-fictional):**

1. **Document, don't fake.** Ship this file. Do **not** add a `--no-watermark`
   flag against Anthropic — it would be a lie (§9.0). A flag that silently
   does nothing is worse than no flag.
2. **Make the honest path first-class.** The lever already exists; the gap
   is *discoverability*. Concretely:
   - Add a one-line note in `print_usage()` under `--provider`:
     *"local/open-weight backends (ollama, llama.cpp) carry no provider
     watermark; hosted Claude output is watermarked per EU AI Act."*
   - Optionally surface a short hint in the provider picker
     (`src/runtime/view/pickers.cpp`) tagging Anthropic/OpenAI/Google rows
     as "watermarked (EU AI Act)" and local rows as "no watermark".
3. **If you truly want a scrub stage** (strongly discouraged for a coding
   agent): it would be a *post-response text transform* on assistant prose
   only, never on code/diffs, and it is exactly the §4 paraphrase attack —
   lossy, model-assisted, and it would change meaning. agentty's value is
   faithful code edits; a paraphrase pass is antithetical to that. Leave it
   out.

**Net:** the way to let watermark-averse users use agentty is to point them
at a **local open-weight model** (§9.2) and make that path obvious in
`--help` and the picker — not to pretend a switch can disable a
server-side, no-opt-out compliance control.

## 8. Bottom line

A **legitimate, invisible, quality-neutral statistical watermark**
(SynthID-Text / Tournament sampling) added for **EU AI Act compliance** —
**not** hidden Unicode, **not** traceable to you, **not** removable by a
paid tier, robust to copy-paste but **not** to paraphrase, and **largely
irrelevant to generated code** because code lacks the entropy to carry it
and formatters erase what little remains. The "watermark removal" tools
selling zero-width-character stripping are solving a myth.
